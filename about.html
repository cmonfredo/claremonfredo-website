about

Clare Monfredo is a cellist, scholar of performance practice, and Research Assistant Professor at the University of Rochester. Across fields, her work is motivated by the same underlying question: how are individual voices expressed across art forms? This question guides her approach to performance, research, curation, and teaching.

As a cellist, Clare has performed stylistically wide-ranging music across the United States and Europe, including with Ensemble Intercontemporain, A Far Cry, Lucerne Contemporary Orchestra, International Contemporary Ensemble, Heartbeat Opera, and Wet Ink Ensemble; alongside jazz musicians including Ron Carter and Jon Batiste; and in collaboration with visual artists including Nene Humphrey and Miya Masaoka. She has performed at an array of New York City venues — from Carnegie Hall to Little Island — and across Europe, including in the Paris Philharmonie, Hamburg Elbphilharmonie, Brussels’ Bozar, Leipzig Gewandhaus, and KKL Luzern. Clare’s festival appearances include Tanglewood Music Center, Music Academy of the West, Norfolk Chamber Music Festival, Aldeburgh Festival (UK), Lucerne Festival (CHE), Piatigorsky International Cello Festival, Chamber Music Northwest, and Kurt Weill Fest (DE). Her primary mentors include Norman Fischer, Peter Bruns, David Geber, Julia Lichten, and Natasha Brofsky, as well as masterclasses with Yo-Yo Ma, Jean-Guihen Queyras, and Paolo Pandolfo.

Clare received a Doctor of Musical Arts with distinction from the City University of New York Graduate Center, where she was the recipient of the Dissertation Fellowship, Provost’s Fellowship, and 5 Year Graduate Center Fellowship. Prior to living in New York she studied in Leipzig, Germany on a Fulbright Scholarship with cellist Peter Bruns, and holds a Bachelor of Arts in English from Yale University, as well as a Master of Music degree from the Shepherd School at Rice University, where she studied with Norman Fischer. Her work has been featured in The Strad and Strings Magazine, and her scholarship has been presented at conferences on music theory, music performance, and timbre studies, including the Society for Music Theory and the International Conference on Timbre.

In addition to her position at the University of Rochester, Clare has taught at Hunter College and Queens College in New York, and teaches music and critical writing privately. A dedicated chamber musician, she is a member of the New York–based chamber group Sonora Collective and is co-founder and co-artistic director of DownEast New Music, Maine’s first chamber music ensemble focused exclusively on contemporary music.

Performance bio

Cellist Clare Monfredo performs stylistically wide-ranging music across the United States and Europe, from Bach to newly commissioned works. She has appeared with Ensemble Intercontemporain, A Far Cry, and International Contemporary Ensemble, alongside jazz musicians including Ron Carter and Jon Batiste, and at venues from Carnegie Hall to the Hamburg Elbphilharmonie. She is co-founder and co-artistic director of DownEast New Music, Maine’s first chamber music ensemble focused exclusively on contemporary music. Also active as a scholar of performance practice, she is Research Assistant Professor at the University of Rochester.

Research bio

Clare Monfredo is a cellist, scholar of performance practice, and Research Assistant Professor at the University of Rochester. Her research examines interpretation and individuality in classical music — how performers negotiate the constraints and freedoms of the tradition — through quantitative and qualitative analysis of recorded performances. Her work has been presented at the Society for Music Theory and the International Conference on Timbre, and she holds a DMA from the CUNY Graduate Center. As a cellist, she has performed across the United States and Europe and co-directs the contemporary ensemble DownEast New Music.
